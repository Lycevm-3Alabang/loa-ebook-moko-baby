### S2 — what the investigation changed

- The plan's S2 had two halves. The scan found **both states are unreachable**: the FKs cascade
  (`rubric_groups → rubric_categories → rubric_items → evaluation_ratings`, all `ON DELETE
  CASCADE`), so a rating cannot outlive its rubric item; and a period has **one** rubric group with
  the category name resolved at read time, so the two alias spellings never coexist in one period's
  figures.
- So the slice landed as the **narrow fix** (the owner's choice): the general rating leaves the
  error bucket out, and the reachable numbers **do not change** (the bucket cannot exist).
- ⭐ **The reachable question the old item never asked** — a criterion the app has **no column for**
  (a school adds a category like "Course Coverage") **is a real criterion**: it counts toward the
  general rating today and no column shows it. That behaviour is **kept**; changing it is a product
  decision, and it is recorded in `TODO.md` rather than decided here.
- The **alias half** was **not** changed: pooling the two spellings exactly needs the per-category
  counts, which the column mapping does not have — a ripple through every caller to fix a state
  the schema forbids. The map stays as the defensive lookup it was written to be.

### S1 — what the investigation changed

- The plan's blast radius for option A said "2 write sites". It is **3**: the restore has to bring the
  state back with the flag, or a restored response would stay out of every figure.
- The restore needs a **DRAFT** branch, because the `invalidate*` methods (unlike the bulk path)
  do not filter `status = 'SUBMITTED'` and can hit a never-submitted row. `submittedAt` is the signal —
  it is set exactly at submit (`evaluations.repository.ts:309`) and there are no seeded evaluations.
- **No DDL.** `'INVALID'` is already a legal value: Migration 23 (`supabase-schema.sql:1707-1712`)
  drops the base `CHECK (status IN ('DRAFT','SUBMITTED'))` and adds the widened one, inside a
  `DO $$ … EXCEPTION` block (AGENTS.md Lesson 2). The four existing `invalidate*` methods write it
  in production today.

## Decisions

### The story is eight independent stories

The scan (below) shows the eight groups share **no** code, **no** invariant and **no** test file
except through two shared contracts. They are not one change; they are eight, and three of them
are **product decisions** the code cannot make. The plan therefore has to be a **sequence of
independent slices**, each with its own decision, not one edit.

### S3 — the slice's own decisions (2026-10-21)

**The rule.** A response is in scope **iff its period is the selected period**
(`evaluations.evaluation_period_id`). The period's **parent semester**
(`evaluation_periods.semesterId`, free — `service.ts:151` loads it in the same
call) scopes the **subject list** only. The assignment's
`faculty_subjects.semesterId` is the **teaching's** stamp and is **not** a
response filter. The department page used the subject list's scope as a
response filter; that single line (`service.ts:190`) is the defect.

**How each `applies` invariant is preserved.**

  `groups/[facultySubjectId]` routes and the two department routes keep

  `resolveSubjectDepartment`), not route logic; the scope rule moves into the
  service and the repository.
- **proxy.ts is the access-enforcement point** — `proxy.ts` — **n/a**: no
  route, page or role change. D9 is a *resource* guard inside a route — the

### S2 — the category arithmetic: the `""` bucket **and** the aliases (one slice, one file)

**Problem.** A rating whose `itemId` no longer resolves becomes the category name `""`
(`lib/db/common.ts:99-110`). `computeGeneralRating` averages over **every** name present → the `""`
bucket **counts**; `mapCategoryAveragesToColumns` drops unknown names → it can **never** be a

row order, so one subject can print two different numbers on two screens.

### S3 — the membership gate: the period scopes the responses

**The rule (owner, 2026-10-21).** A response is in scope **iff its period is the selected period**
