Why the two methods differ (the drift mechanism):
- Pooled (detail + faculty PDF): every rating row of the faculty enters, then the 8 category
  averages are averaged (lib/evaluation-utils.ts:58 -> :72). Each RESPONSE weighs equally.
- Card: each SUBJECT weighs equally, because it averages already-averaged subject numbers.
  Faculty with subject A (100 responses, avg 4.0) and subject B (5 responses, avg 2.0):
  card = 3.00, pooled = ~3.8. The card and its own PDF disagree by that much.
- Second-order: computeGeneralRating averages only the categories PRESENT in the group
  (lib/evaluation-utils.ts:74). A category missing for subject A but present for subject B is
  included in the pooled faculty value and absent from subject A's own value - so even a
  response-weighted mean of subject averages is NOT the pooled value.
- Third: both the department widget (page.tsx:75-77 deptAvg) and the faculty card average
  means-of-means; the department widget's "Total Respondents" (page.tsx:73) sums per-subject
  respondent counts, so a student who rated 3 subjects is counted 3x.

Proof: none for the faculty-level aggregate. lib/__tests__/evaluation-results-service.test.ts
covers only the stale-eval filter; lib/__tests__/evaluation-utils.test.ts covers the pure math;
no test covers the faculty card or client aggregation. Command: npm test

Teach (6=A): why it behaves this way - the department page and the detail page never share one
aggregator. The detail page and the consolidated-faculty route both pool every rating row of the
group and then average the 8 category averages. The "By Faculty" card instead averages the
already-averaged per-subject numbers on the client, because the department API only returns
per-subject rows and the card was written to have something to display without another request.
Averaging means-of-means is not the same as pooling, so the card number and the number in the
PDF the card itself downloads disagree. Why now - the card is the only surface where a faculty's
headline number is shown without opening a subject, so it is the only place the drift is visible
as a contradiction. When it matters - any faculty with more than one subject and unequal
response counts. One alternative - compute the faculty average client-side as a response-weighted
mean of the subject averages; cheaper, but it still disagrees with the pooled number because of
the missing-category effect above.
